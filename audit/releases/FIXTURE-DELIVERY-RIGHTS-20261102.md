## Proposed public presentation threat model

Threats are anonymous scraping, ordinary-account scraping, altered filters/date/ID/cursors, direct base/view/RPC access, forged user-editable role claims, public-function privilege escalation, oversized or poisoned presentation payloads and unintended cache/raw-history fallback. Rate limits and a hidden frontend control alone do not authorize raw data.

The candidate design is a finite, server-selected current presentation edition with closed screen/field contracts, no arbitrary fixture query, cursor or historical corpus download; stored history and unchanged engine computation remain behind explicit server-authorized publisher/research access. Public readers receive permitted display facts plus genuinely published/derived forecasts, never the fitting corpus or original research packets. The exact implementation and test disposition are recorded in the private final review, once the candidate is frozen. This design statement alone is not evidence that a restriction is implemented.

Even a correctly bounded public edition is inspectable JSON. Repeated observations can retain the facts actually displayed. Client origin, obscurity, CORS, an anon key, account login, response caps and request throttles cannot prevent a determined reader from recording those facts. No code can promise that a browser-delivered display fact is impossible to extract. The distinction between necessary transport for the permitted app and a prohibited separate feed requires a **specific rights decision**. The engineering remedy can close broad historical/raw access without claiming that this legal ambiguity has disappeared.

## Exact candidate public transport for the rights decision

This is the proposed interface actually implemented in the draft, not a hypothetical unrestricted REST view. The only public presentation selector is one of `scores`, `predictions`, `history`, `records`, or `slips`. The server chooses one currently valid UTC-day edition; callers cannot supply a fixture, competition, date, field set, filter, cursor or offset to fetch other source records. The maximum document is512KiB with at most1000 fixture cards/board rows and128 issued slips; overflow or missing required original prediction inventory fails publication instead of silently discarding rows. These are technical bounds, not licensing thresholds.

- Fixture cards: fixture/competition IDs and names, country, kickoff date/time, status/elapsed, home/away names, final/halftime/fulltime components with explicit nulls, stored source-update timestamp and provider label. No source response, packed history, team or player statistical corpus, injuries, lineups, tactical dataset, crest/logo or provider credential is included. Timestamps are retained stored fields, not a new claim of original provider receipt or freshness.
- Original published prediction subset: the original team/competition identity and original pick/market/probability, Home/Draw/Away vector, model-produced scoring rates, original kickoff/issue/withdrawal timestamps, final-pick marker and build identity. These are projections of retained immutable records, not re-estimates from the public card set. All required original prediction rows must be accounted for; no record is regraded or rewritten by the serving proposal.
- Derived board: current published pick, probability and Home/Draw/Away values with fixture identity, gating/frozen state, model-produced scoring rates and board publication metadata. A newer board cannot replace an older frozen issued claim. The server's current-edition timestamp is a materialization clock, not proof that the provider updated a price, score or source at that time.
- Engine/CLV records: pre-existing aggregate counts and summary statistics in a closed versioned shape. Arbitrary `shared_records` payloads/keys are not forwarded.
- Issued-slip subset: existing issued selection identity, probability, combined price and any selected leg price, plus relevant fixture identity/date and build/step. This is not a general odds endpoint. **The supplied provider correspondence did not expressly settle bookmaker-price or onward-sharing rights; these selected prices remain a specific rights question.**

The materializer privately selects stored fixtures in its fixed current -6/+7day Scores window, every retained immutable prediction, every current published board fixture and the fixtures belonging to the recent31day issued-slip inventory. Scores returns only -6/+7day cards with nested predictions removed; Predictions returns the fixed -1/+7day/current-board membership; History returns issued prediction and issued-slip result membership; Records and Slips omit fixture-card inventories. The client may combine only fixed Scores/History responses from the same edition for legitimate historical result joins; it never sends caller fixture IDs to expand the server inventory. A reader can still inspect each disclosed screen and retain successive editions. This is reduced exposure, not a claim that scraping has become impossible.

The targeted question above should include this exact field/window summary and additionally ask whether display of already-issued selection prices is permitted and whether the public JSON path must have any specific account, field, cache, audience or onward-sharing restriction. It must not imply the provider approved an endpoint it has not reviewed.

The inspected `ai-analyze` cache is model/web-derived text/context according to its actual writer, not proven direct API-Football raw rows. Retained cache record contents and sources were not read. The separate draft Edge guard limits fixture cache access to trusted source users or the current presentation membership and validates a closed, bounded cached response; this does not independently authenticate stored statements or confer third-party rights. Existing numerical context fields are preserved, not newly validated as predictive adjustments or enabled as an A4 mapping.

The draft collector hook refreshes the bounded edition after existing scheduler-authorized collection/write paths, and the publisher hook follows the authoritative board publication. Neither public display RPC triggers collection or model computation. The collector hook is one database RPC with no retry and reports an unavailable projection independently of the already-committed raw-source operation. The actual Edge source patches and SQL proposal remain unapplied; real secret wiring, collection cadence, source freshness and deployment compatibility require separate acceptance.
